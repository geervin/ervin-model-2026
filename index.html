06   TRANSPARENCY & REPRODUCIBILITY

What these predictions actually mean

01 / Historical correction

In D-versus-R races: Adjusted D–R = current poll D–R − mean(past poll margin − past actual margin). Positive historical errors mean Republicans outperformed polling. The original Ervin ±5 directional rule is retained. An optional small-sample shrinkage toggle multiplies the historical correction by n/(n+3), yielding a separate sensitivity scenario.

02 / Evidence grades & intervals

Historical mean absolute error (MAE) is computed from the election-by-election audit. Grade thresholds include sample-size requirements. Uncertainty bands use a conservative scenario formula with a ≥4.7-point historical dispersion floor and wider bands for few elections, fallbacks, ranked-choice ballots and independents. These are not formally calibrated statistical confidence intervals or win probabilities.

03 / RealClearPolling benchmark

RCP is the comparison reference; a separate source tab is never blended with RCP. If a publisher lacks an identifiable same-matchup polling average, we use a named polling proxy and flag it at the row and in the race detail. We do not relabel an outside estimate as a genuine RCP/DDHQ/270toWin average. Different sources can have different field dates.

Overlap warning: Wikipedia's average-of-aggregators uses aggregator averages that may contain many of the same underlying surveys. This is not equivalent to a unique-poll sample or five independent observations. Uneven calibration: all candidate fields and historical samples are documented, including mixed historical RCP/Wikipedia inputs inherited from the working model. Independent races and ranked-choice voting require more caution.
